Does your agent rot
as its context fills?

Everyone says coding agents get worse as the window fills. contextrot checks that claim against the sessions already on your disk — and finds what actually moves your failure rate.

01 · the window

Every step, your agent rereads everything so far.

Every file it opened, every tool result, every message — replayed on each turn. That's the context window.

01 · the window

It fills. Fast.

A long session passes hundreds of thousands of tokens. Most of it is history.

01 · the folklore

Past ~60%, the advice goes, it starts to rot.

/clear often. Keep it small. Split work into subagents. Compact early.

01 · the source

That advice comes from lab benchmarks.

Needle-in-a-haystack. Synthetic retrieval. Good studies — but not your repo, not your prompts, not your work.

01 · the gap

Nobody had checked it on real sessions.

The data was on your disk the whole time. Every agent CLI logs every step — token counts, tool calls, errors.

02 · how it measures

Five ways an agent slips.

For every step in every session, contextrot records how full the window was — and whether one of five things went wrong. Then it asks one question: does the rate climb as the window fills?

edit_failure

The edit didn't apply

It aimed a change at text that isn't there — wrong line, stale copy, invented context.

retry

Same call, again

An identical tool call right after the last one. It didn't learn from the answer.

reread

Reading what it already has

Opening a file that's still in context, unchanged. Lost track of its own window.

tool_error

The tool said no

A command that failed, a path that doesn't exist, arguments that don't parse.

self_correction

"I apologize, let me fix that"

The agent noticing, in its own words, that the last thing it did was wrong.

and the statistics

0%

Wilson confidence intervals on every rate. A threshold is declared only when the evidence clears the baseline — one noisy bucket can't scare you.

05 · contextrot factors

So what does move it?

Context fill is one suspect. contextrot factors runs the same statistics across five more and ranks what genuinely separates your good steps from your bad ones. Click a row to see every group.

clearmaybeno effect

    Association, not causation. Ordered factors compare their two ends; categories compare best against worst. Real data is one developer — models and agents there did different jobs.

    time of day · real data

    5.1% night · 0–6h

    Slips 1.6× as often after midnight as in the morning. Almost certainly the human, not the model — tired prompts, riskier asks. The most actionable thing it found.
